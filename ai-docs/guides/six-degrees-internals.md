# Six Degrees internals

Reference for work on Six Degrees: the `connection*` services, `wikidata*` / `wikiTitles`, portraits, `ConnectionExplorer` and `src/components/connectionNetwork/`. The guardrails in `AGENTS.md` apply on top of everything here.

## Components

`ConnectionPicker` chooses the other end, `ConnectionPanel` is the sidebar Connection tab, `ConnectionThread` draws the SVG thread, `ConnectionStage` plays a finished connection over the blurred canvas (it has the whole viewport height: while it is shown `App` passes `away` to the canvas header (`ControlPanel`) and the category `Legend`, which slide up and leave the tab order, and `fullHeight` to the `Sidebar`, which then reaches the top, so only the right inset for the open sidebar is left; its header carries the degrees, a bare Replay play icon, a bare Open exploration icon (`ExplorationIcon`, the sidebar card's, shown when `savedEntryFor(recent, chain)` finds the saved entry: `useConnection.activeEntry` / `openActiveExploration`, which takes over from a reveal that is playing like Why does) and the round Close button), `SixDegreesInfo` is the "?" dialog explaining Six Degrees and this app's twist (chains across centuries), and `ConnectionExplorer` is the live contact-network overlay (graph, inspector, footer).

## AI operations

- `fetchConnectionPath()`: one bounded Six Degrees seed proposal; failure falls through to graph exploration.
- `resolveFigure()`: identify one typed name as a person, or `null`.
- `discoverPersonalContacts()`, `assessPersonalContacts()`, `explainPersonalContact()`: dedicated Six Degrees operations in Gemini, OpenRouter and Ollama, with provider-neutral prompts/parsers in `connectionAI.ts`. Only independently accepted contacts enter a chain; exact opponents in a named battle/campaign qualify.
- `fetchRelationshipExplanation()` keeps the legacy `{ contactOnly: true }` contract available; the Six Degrees engine and Why action do not use it.
- Why dialog: bypasses the general negative caches and falls back to compact accepted evidence if narrative expansion fails. It is as full as the general relationship dialog: `buildContactExplanationPrompt` reuses the general prompt's narrative rules verbatim (`relationshipNarrativeRules`: summary, 3-5 sections, 250-450 words, engaging titles) after a Context section, treats the contact as already verified so the model does not answer minimally, and `requestContactExplanation` asks once more when the answer has fewer than 3 narrative sections or about 160 words (a thin second answer is still used). The header cards load their Wikipedia portrait and biography alongside the narrative, and `recordedContactSection` always adds the stored evidence (the text the Connection tab shows beside the link) as the first section.

## Persistence

- Shared database: `chronoweave-six-degrees-v2`, outside canvas localStorage cleanup. Accepted contacts retain provider/model/version/timestamp provenance and remain reusable across models.
- New successful paths: `chrono_sd2_path_`, retaining their own identities and compact accepted evidence for replay even after knowledge eviction.
- Guests: `settleConnection` weaves a chain's new people in as guests of that connection (`weaveConnectionIntoCanvas`, `src/utils/connectionGuests.ts`). `CanvasConfig.guests` maps a figure id to the saved path keys that brought it in; chain members already on the canvas stay core, except that one who is already a guest of another connection gets this key too. Weaving widens only the year range: `weaveContext.activeCategories`, the category filter and discovery clusters are untouched, and `normalizeSnapshot` exempts guests from the category drop instead. The filter bar offers the categories that have a card on the canvas, guests' included (`availableCategories` in `App.tsx`). Both canvases lay guests out apart, under a "From connections" header (`calculateGalleryLayout` with `guestIds`; `calculateCanvasTimelineLayout`, a band after one empty row `guestRow`); the legend's show/dim/hide control is `CanvasViewSnapshot.guestVisibility`. Cards of everyone on a saved connection (its ends and links, on the main canvas or guests) carry a chip of the paired end portraits of the newest one, over the portrait's corner (`ConnectionPair` with `chip`, a "+N" badge for the others, a "From N connections" `Tooltip`; `figureConnections` resolves every saved path with `connectionPathEntry`, so evicted paths and `'legacy'` guests show none), and timeline labels show the same chip before the name. Clicking a chip (`data-connection-chip`, found by the canvas's hit test) opens `ConnectionMenu` instead of the actions: those connections, each played with `useConnection.replay`. A guest's action menu has no View Relationship (the general check against the focus figure does not apply to them) and no Expand Timeline. "Move to Main Canvas" is reversible (`moveToMain` / `moveBackToConnections`): the id stays in `guests` and joins `movedToMain`, so it is laid out with everyone else (`activeGuestIds` = guests minus moved) and offers "Move Back to Connections"; a category the move had to add to `activeCategories` is recorded in `movedCategories` and leaves both again once no main-canvas person has it. Deleting a connection never removes someone moved to the main canvas: they just stop being guests. The Delete icon on a Recent connections card (with an inline confirm) calls `useConnection.deleteConnection`: it removes the path key, drops the guests no other connection needs from the canvas and from `chrono_woven_ids` (`deleteConnectionPath`), and closes the connection if it is on show. Eviction by `MAX_RECENT_CONNECTIONS` never removes guests. Canvases saved without a `guests` map are migrated once on load (`migrateGuests`): woven ids still on the canvas become guests of the saved paths they are on, or of `'legacy'`; `activeCategories` widened by past weaves is left as it is.
- Legacy `chrono_path_` / `chrono_contact_` records are not imported or shown as new connections. Migration logically retires them without deletion and preserves the legacy path/woven membership union under `chrono_sd2_members_`; failed writes leave original protection intact. Run migration before Follow a Figure pruning on activation.
- Saved sessions: a checkpoint never refuses a long search. `fitSession` cuts one over `KNOWLEDGE_LIMITS.sessionPeople`/`sessionPairs` (5000/12000) down to fit, keeping endpoints, the accepted network, time, rounds and counters and dropping blocked, then proposed, then unresolved contacts (and people with none left) first; before that, sessions over 1024 people / 4096 contacts silently stopped saving and reopening showed an older copy. The running session carries a transient `saveIssue` (`'trimmed'`, or `'failed'` when saving worked before but the write failed), shown as a warning in the explorer's status area and never saved.
- Deleting a paused exploration: the Delete icon on a Paused explorations card (inline confirm, like Recent connections) calls `useConnection.deleteCheckpoint` → `ConnectionRepository.deleteSession(key)`, which removes only the saved session. The shared knowledge (people and contacts, accepted or unresolved, with provenance) is deliberately kept: a later search for that pair, or any other, reuses it instead of asking the model again; only the search state (candidates, rounds, attempts, counters) is lost. The delete is queued behind any checkpoint write in flight, is ignored while a search runs, and never touches the canvas, saved paths or guests.
- Reopening a made connection: the icon on a Recent connections card (and on Paused explorations, where the whole card opens it too) calls `useConnection.openSavedExploration`, which uses `finishedExploration` (the saved search of the pair when the store still holds every link of the chain, otherwise a small one rebuilt from the saved path's `identityIds`/`people`/`contacts`). The explorer opens with `solved` (not `success`): the whole network is shown and explorable, nothing is dimmed or framed, the degrees badge replaces the arrow between the endpoint portraits, Reveal replaces the phase text in the header and plays the connection on the canvas (`revealSolved`), the search buttons are gone, and `chain` forces which route is the solved one. Its edges and rings are lime (`--color-chain-path`), also with the lines hidden (then only the chain's lines are drawn); with the lines shown every other line recedes to 30% (`is-solved`); the just-found `success` state keeps its centred banner, dimming and framing, with the same lime.

## Search

- Defaults in `SEARCH_LIMITS` are tunable; keep the selected 21-link bound, AI-only assessment, cross-model reuse and explicit continuation rules intact.
- Time gaps: `temporalGap` (`connectionGraph.ts`) is the smallest distance in years between a person each endpoint's side has reached, when no pair from the two sides overlaps in time (null otherwise: then a contact, not a period, is missing). `selectFrontier` puts the people nearest that gap first (within 30 years of the best, expanded fewer than 3 times, nearest first), then rotates as before; with a gap of 40+ years `findConnection` also passes `gap: { fromYear, toYear }` to `discoverPersonalContacts`, and `buildContactDiscoveryPrompt` adds a preference for contacts whose lives reach into that period (never a reason to invent one). Per-request cost and the golden request trace are unchanged by it; the fake worlds never exercise it, `tests/connectionGap.test.ts` does.
- Request accounting: `ConnectionSession.diagnostics` carries optional counters, absent in sessions saved before them and whitelisted again in `recoverSession`. Per segment: `requests` by kind (`seed`, `direct` = the endpoint check's assessment request, `assess`, `discover`, `identity`; they sum to `tasks`), `assessBatches` and `discoverSources` (sizes per request, in order), `pairAsks` by reason (`fresh`, `focused`, `contextChange`) and `validationRetries` (best-effort correction rounds, a delta of the counter in `jsonResponse.ts` around `task()`); `total` is the whole session. Counting never changes batching, limits, prompts or retries: `tests/connectionRequestTrace.golden.json` pins the request sequence of the fake worlds; change it only for a deliberate behavior change.

## Wikidata identity

`src/services/wikidataService.ts`, `wikiTitles.ts`.

- A person entering the store is resolved to a Wikidata item: the name as a Wikipedia title (redirect-aware, so "Robert Oppenheimer" and "J. Robert Oppenheimer" are one item), else `wbsearchentities`; the statements come from one SPARQL query per 50 QIDs (one row per statement; SPARQL numbers BCE years astronomically, `historicalYear` converts to the app's no-year-zero convention).
- Acceptance (`acceptIdentity`): a QID is accepted only for a human whose Wikidata years are within `DUPLICATE_DATE_TOLERANCE_YEARS` (10) of the proposed ones (or, when one of the two years is within `NEAR_MATCH_YEARS` (2), the other within `NEAR_MATCH_TOLERANCE_YEARS` (25): a model that got one end of a life wrong, such as Pierre Bourdieu as 1919 instead of 1930, is still the same person; a namesake would have to die in the same year too; a miss recorded before `IDENTITY_RULES_UPDATED_AT` is asked again at once) and with at least one trustworthy year (deprecated and coarser-than-year statements ignored, preferred rank wins, statements that still disagree are settled by the year nearest the proposed one, a human with no death statement is alive and dies this year); anything else leaves the person exactly as the model gave them.
- `ContactPerson` gains `qid`, `variants` (up to 6 other name-and-years pairs the person was recorded or proposed under; `samePerson` matches through them, which is how canvas figures and saved paths that still carry the model's old years find a corrected person, so none is woven in twice) and `qidCheckedAt` (a miss is asked again after 30 days; a failed lookup is recorded nowhere). Ids are never re-keyed, and the canvas is never modified.
- Merging: records of one QID merge with `mergeResolvedIdentity` (`chooseSurvivor`: an endpoint, else the record whose years were already right, else the most valid accepted contacts, else the first recorded; a merge also drops self-pairs and de-duplicates contacts); contacts that stop fitting after a date correction stay stored and `validAccepted` ignores them. Same-name namesakes with different QIDs are still excluded from routes (`ambiguousNames`).
- In the search: `findConnection` takes an optional `wikidata` resolver (absent: identification exactly as before, which is why the golden trace of the older worlds is unchanged). With it, endpoints, seed proposals, canvas references and discovery candidates are resolved in one batch each before they are identified, the people already known are reconciled at the start and whenever an accepted contact brings new ones in (`reconcileIdentities`), and the model's `identity` requests (`resolveDuplicate`, alias collisions) are skipped when QIDs already decide. Wikidata is never a `task()`: no request counter moves, a failure or a cancelled lookup never fails the search, and `diagnostics.wikidata` counts lookups, resolved, merged, corrected and failed.
- Opening a saved exploration (`useConnection.openCheckpoint` / `openSavedExploration`) runs `reconcileSession` in the background and swaps in the corrected one; saved paths (Recent connections) stay snapshots.
- Testing: `scripts/wikidataSmoke.ts` checks the real APIs by hand; the tests use real responses recorded in `tests/fixtures/wikidata/`.

## Portraits

- Sizing: figures store 400px Wikimedia thumbnails; every display passes the URL through `sizedPortrait(url, boxCssPixels, zoom)` (`src/utils/portraitSize.ts`), which asks Wikimedia for the smallest standard width covering the box at the current zoom and pixel ratio (never wider than stored). Six Degrees contact portraits are fetched at 120px directly. Hundreds of oversized pictures overflow the browser's decoded-image cache and make every repaint decode them again.
- Lookups are remembered across page loads: every portrait goes through `fetchBatchWikipediaImages` (`wikiService.ts`), which asks `portraitStore()` (`src/services/portraitStore.ts`, IndexedDB database `chronoweave-portraits`, key `width|name`, a URL or a miss, memory-only where there is no IndexedDB) first and sends only the unknown or expired names to Wikipedia (hits expire after 90 days, misses after 7), in chunks of 50, three at a time. Only a real answer is stored: a failed request, a non-OK status, a reply without pages or an unreachable Wikidata fallback is "unanswered" (`askWikipediaForImages` returns `answered`) and is asked again next time, never recorded as "no picture". The widths are kept apart (400 canvas, 120 Six Degrees). `fetchBatchFigureDetails`, `fetchWikipediaImagesByName` and `loadNetworkPortraits` take an `onStored` callback that hands over the remembered portraits before the newcomers are answered (the gallery, sidebar, Connection tab and explorer hooks use it), and a figure that already has `imageUrl` is not looked up. Tests inject a store with `setPortraitStore`.
- The URLs are deliberately not stored on `ContactPerson` or `HistoricalFigure`: that would need whitelisting in `recoverKnowledge`, surviving `merge` and `mergeResolvedIdentity`, and writing the canvas back on every portrait.
- Who gets a Six Degrees face (`portraitSubjects`, `connectionNetworkView.ts`): AI-supported people by name, as above; an unverified person never by name (a namesake's face could land on a person the search does not trust), except when Wikidata has confirmed who they are, i.e. they have a `qid`: then the subject carries the `qid` and `fetchPortraitsByQid` (`wikiService.ts`; one SPARQL `wdt:P18` query per 50 items, `portraitsByQid` in `wikidataService.ts`) gives the picture Wikidata holds for exactly that item, remembered in the same store as `qid:Q…` at 120px. Unverified people without a `qid` (Wikidata not asked yet, or no answer) and items without a P18 keep the dashed initials disc (`NetworkPortrait` with `tentative`, which only styles: whether a url exists is decided by the subjects). The Inspector's "Unverified around this person" rows show it too, and `ConnectionExplorer` offers the selected person's unverified contacts even when they are not drawn. `loadNetworkPortraits` keeps the two routes apart (`id` vs `id|qid` in its cache), so a person who becomes AI-supported is then looked up by name.

## Explorer graph

- Performance: the graph layout runs in a worker (`src/workers/networkLayout.worker.ts` hosting `NetworkLayout` through `networkLayoutHost.ts`); `RemoteNetworkLayout` mirrors it on the main thread (people, time axis, optimistic pins, latest frame, the dragged person at the pointer) behind `NetworkLayoutHandle`, one frame request in flight, and `createNetworkLayout` falls back to an in-thread `NetworkLayout` where there is no `Worker` (tests). People without a position yet stay hidden. `NetworkGraph` renders lines and people as memoized layers (camera, clock and axis changes do not re-render them) and paints positions directly; edges are `<path>`s (one `d` each). From `LARGE_DRAWING` drawn items the `is-large` class turns off the opacity fade on dimming: those per-element SVG transitions were what made selecting someone stutter.
- Free zones: on the Timeline and Free layouts `NetworkLayout.keepClear` keeps everyone out of a free zone around each endpoint (3 endpoint radii plus the person's own radius and `clearance`; vertical push on the time line, radial in Free; zones shrink together, to a floor, when the endpoints are close). The arranged layouts have none.
- Timeline layout: a person stands at the middle of their lifetime (`xOf(year)`, a soft pull) and `keepInLife` (after every `tick`) never lets them leave the lifetime: each `LayoutInput` carries `reach`, half the lifespan in years, and x is held within `year ± reach` while the crowd makes room above and below instead. The person being dragged is free; endpoints are fixed. Dragging on the time line and in the Free layout (both are `calm` layouts) does not reheat the simulation (a hot, packed crowd shifted as a whole and cooled into overlaps): the dragged person collides with the crowd, so the people beside them give way, and everybody is drawn back (`anchorForce`, `ANCHOR_PULL`) to the place they rested at (`anchors`, taken when the first drag starts and kept from drag to drag until the graph's people or the layout change), so the crowd closes up behind the drag and what an endpoint pushed aside goes back when the endpoint is moved away. A dropped person homes to their year with a force of their own (`homeForce`) and becomes anchored there. Once an endpoint is let go, `keepClear` puts anybody it carried past (a fast drag jumps over people) back on the side their anchor is on (in the Free layout: in the direction of their anchor). In the Free layout nothing homes (a dropped person stays pinned where they were let go) and the links no longer pull neighbours along during a drag (the heat that drove them is gone; the people beside the dragged one are pushed by collision). The time line and the Free layout also keep ticking past `ALPHA_MIN`, on collisions alone, until nobody moves faster than `REST_SPEED` (at most `REST_MAX_TICKS`, twice that after a drop), since stopping at `ALPHA_MIN` left the crowd mid-slide; collision runs 2 iterations there. People who leave the graph reheat the time line and the free layout too, so whoever they had pushed aside goes back.
- Layout help: the "?" button (last in the graph's bottom-left toolbar, `LayoutInfo`) explains the layout in use. `LAYOUT_INFO` holds the wording per `LayoutMode`; a new layout needs an entry there and a picture in `Diagram`.
- Footer: shows `NetworkView.sides` (counts with a hover tooltip, hint text in the middle column, buttons on the right), the accepted people nearer to each endpoint (same rule as node colours, endpoints excluded, filters and folds ignored).
- Legend: every chip is a switch, the AI-supported one included (`showAccepted` in `buildNetworkView`, default on). Off, the graph draws the endpoints, the people and contacts of the chips that are on, whatever is selected, and the lit chain (`highlightChain` or `chain`: a just-found or solved connection) with its people; the inspector's lists, counts and side counts do not change with it. A found or solved connection adds a lime "Connection" chip, also a switch (`showChain`, default on, reset per exploration): off, the chain is no longer lit, dimming the rest or kept drawn when the AI-supported contacts are off, and the graph reads like an ordinary exploration. While the chain is lit in a solved connection the AI-supported swatch is neutral (`is-neutral`, as the faded accepted lines are); otherwise it is amber like the lines.
- Clicking lines: `edgeDrawOrder` (`connectionNetworkView.ts`) draws the lines faded by a selection first, then the focused ones (a selected person's lines, the selected contact), the lit chain last of each, so where lines cross a click lands on the one on top, the one being looked at; those lines also have a wider hit area (`.net-edge-hit`). People are drawn over all lines. Panning: a drag that starts on a line pans the canvas like one on empty space (lines cover a crowded graph); the pointer is captured only once it has moved past `DRAG_SLOP`, because a captured pointer would swallow the click that selects the line, and a click on a line never clears the selection. People and the "+n" badges still take their own gestures.

## Explorer sidebar

- Windowed lists: the People / Contacts tabs of `NetworkInspector` hold hundreds or thousands of rows. `buildListRows` (`inspectorRows.ts`) turns a tab into one flat array of rows (person, contact, group header, empty message; the Unverified groups are header rows that fold their members in and out, `openGroups`, and only an open group is sorted and listed) and `VirtualList` puts only the rows near the viewport in the document (rows `position: absolute` at a summed offset, `overscan` rows beyond the viewport, `aria-setsize`/`aria-posinset` on each `<li>`, natural Tab order). Heights are fixed per kind (`ROW_HEIGHT`, the 6px gap included) instead of measured, so scrolling does no layout reads and nothing jumps; the text is clamped to fit (one line, two for a contact's "A ↔ B", full text in the button's `title`).
- Scroll position: the list returns to the top when `resetKey` (exploration, tab, search) changes; otherwise the row at the top stays where it is when rows are inserted above it (people found while a search runs). A hidden scroller (the selected person's detail view, the closed bottom sheet) measures zero and keeps its last position until the `ResizeObserver` sees it again.
- Person detail view: windowed the same way, with two tabs (`DetailTab`: AI-supported / Unverified; a person who is not AI-supported has only the second, `detailTabFor`; each person starts on the first) instead of two lists one below the other: `buildDetailRows`, `DETAIL_ROW_HEIGHT`, scroller `.net-detail-scroll`, `resetKey` includes the selected person and the tab.
- Back: always returns to the previous step. Opening a contact from a person's lists pushes the person (with the detail tab and scroll the reader had) onto `ConnectionExplorer`'s `history` (`selectionHistory.ts`: `pushStep`, `backLabel`); selecting on the graph or clearing starts the history over. `back()` pops it (re-selects the person, bumps `restored` so `NetworkInspector` reopens their list on the same tab and `VirtualList` at the same scroll row), and with nothing to pop clears the selection (main lists: their tab and search persist, the scroll is kept by `VirtualList` across being hidden). The button says where it goes (`← Alpha`, `← People`, `← Contacts`) and Esc does the same step before it clears a search or closes; the sheet's × still clears everything. In full screen Esc does not step back: see Explorer full screen.
- Compact top: one stats line, the hint as the tooltip of a "?", the endpoints as two chips, a small person head, so the lists get the height.

## Explorer full screen

Plan and decisions: `ai-docs/plans/six-degrees/real-fullscreen-graph.md`. UI only: no request, prompt or search behavior is involved.

- Maximize is real full screen on wide screens (narrow ones never maximize). It fills the viewport with CSS (`.connection-explorer.is-fullscreen`: no padding, no 1720px cap); the browser Fullscreen API is deliberately not used, because browsers swallow Esc in it and the Esc ladder below would break. The remembered choice (`maximized` in `networkViewPrefs`) is unchanged, so the explorer can reopen straight into full screen.
- The header, footer, top-right X and the bare maximize icon are not rendered in full screen. Their parts are `CompactBar` (`connectionNetwork/`), a floating bar in the glass of the view controls (`.canvas-view-toolbar.ce-bar`), rendered by `ConnectionExplorer` inside the network section and **before** the graph (every person and line of the graph is a tab stop, so a bar after it would be unreachable by keyboard and the dialog's initial focus would land on an SVG node). It exists before the first session too, so a remembered full screen can always be left. Contents, from `barPhase()` (`barPhase.ts`: opening, searching, paused, found, solved): the two endpoint portraits with their counts (`SideCount`, shared with the footer, the hover text starting with name · years · occupation), the degrees pill once connected, the compact `ExplorerClock` (sweeping glass and pulse while searching; the phase text is only its hover text and a hidden `role="status"`), the phase's action as an icon (Stop; Continue/Resume and Start a new search; Reveal for found and solved), a warning icon only when `session.saveIssue` is set, then the panel icon, Leave full screen and Close. Progress messages are not displayed. Opening straight into a remembered full screen starts focus on the dialog itself (`useModalFocus` with `'container'`), not on its first control, which is a portrait or the clock; and the shared `Tooltip` opens on focus only when `isFocusVisible` (keyboard focus), so focus the page moves after a mouse click never pops a tooltip up: hover does.
- The inspection panel floats at the right (`NetworkInspector` with `floating`: `.is-floating`, 380px, hidden when closed but mounted, so its tab, search and scroll survive). `ConnectionExplorer.panelOpen` is its own state: any selection (graph click, or from its lists) opens it; Esc, its × and a click on empty graph space close it; the bar's icon toggles it (on the lists when nothing is selected). Hiding it keeps the selection (the highlight stays); entering full screen with a selection starts it open.
- Esc ladder (`escapeStep`, `escapeLadder.ts`): in full screen, hide the panel, then leave full screen; the selection-back, sheet and search steps are skipped there (none is visible, so a press would look dead). Outside full screen it is unchanged: contact → person → lists, clear the search, close. The Back button inside the panel still steps back.
- The panel never resizes the graph (no relayout). `NetworkGraph`'s `inset` (`FLOATING_PANEL_INSET`, `metrics.ts`) is the strip at the right edge it covers: `fitCamera`, `centerOn` and `visibleOnScreen` use the width that is left, so something picked from the lists is panned out from under the panel. Opening the panel does not move the camera. In CSS, `--panel-inset` moves what is centred in the graph (the bar, the Connected / Search paused / Search interrupted cards) and the legend row out of the panel's way; the cards stand below the bar in full screen.
