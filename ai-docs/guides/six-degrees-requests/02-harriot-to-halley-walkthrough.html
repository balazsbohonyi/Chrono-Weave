Thomas Harriot to Edmond Halley: six degrees, two segments, 338 seconds

A real search from your canvas history. Harriot (1560–1621) and Halley (1656–1742) never overlapped, so no direct check was possible and the engine had to build a bridge of people whose lives overlap pairwise.

Where this comes from. A read-only copy of one saved session, taken 2026-10-14 from the knowledge store of IndexedDB chronoweave-six-degrees-v2, plus the saved chain in chrono_canvas_history. Every request ran on ollama/gpt-oss:120b-cloud. The session stores states, not events, so the replay below is placed by stored fields (measured), and order inside a segment is inferred.

1. The answer: seven people, six links

Three links were already known from earlier searches and cost nothing. Three were assessed during this search, and two of those three needed a second look to become accepted.

The evidence text is what gpt-oss:120b-cloud wrote for each pair. The app does not check it against sources; accuracy is still unverified.

3. What the 12 requests of segment 2 were doing

Segment 2 recorded 12 tasks and 43 pair-assessments over 157.8 s, which is 13.1 s per task. The stored state does not say which task was which, so the split below is reconstructed.

Assessment6 to 8 inferred12 minus 2 identity, minus the discovery and seed requests. At least 6 are needed for 43 pair-asks when every batch holds 8, so the packing was at most 2 requests worse than perfect.

The lab's model uses 2 discovery + 2 identity + about 6 assessment + 2 “unattributed”, which reproduces 12 requests, 43 pair-asks and 159 s.

Per end: who asked for what

You asked how many requests go out for each of the two figures. Discovery is the only step that belongs to one end; assessment is one shared pool. People are assigned to an end by whose frontier they were on (Halley’s reused cluster, or Harriot’s own discovered contacts), so the attribution is inferred; the expansion counts come straight from the stored rounds.
