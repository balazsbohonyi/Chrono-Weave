# Gallery canvas layout

Each successful Weave build, including Surprise Me, defaults to gallery when it contains 1–12 eligible figures/events. Larger datasets run the existing timeline placement engine without painting it. `measureTimelineDensityBounds` measures the resulting figure positions using consistent 320 × 116 pixel card footprints. Short events use their floating-label positions. Lifespan bar lengths, unwrapped title widths, and empty configured year-range padding are excluded from this measurement: they can otherwise make a one-figure-per-row stack look horizontal.

For more than 12 cards, the mode decision is `distributionHeight > distributionWidth`: taller distributions use gallery; equality and wider distributions use timeline. Timeline positions still come from its collision engine at 10 pixels per year. There is no lane-count or viewport-height threshold. Small canvases such as the three-person Marco Polo result use gallery regardless of aspect ratio.

The chosen `layoutMode`, algorithm `layoutVersion`, and `layoutSelection` (automatic/manual) are saved in `chrono_timeline_config`. Discovery, filters, resize, and reload preserve a current decision. Explicit manual choices also survive policy upgrades. Older small timelines without a recorded manual choice receive the gallery default once on restore; version-2 larger canvases keep their existing decisions, and existing galleries stay galleries. Caches predating selection tracking are treated as automatic. Legacy caches without a mode receive one decision when restored. A failed build preserves the existing canvas and mode; a successful new build starts with automatic selection.

Once the canvas has figures, a filled circular button at the bottom left switches between gallery and the year-based timeline. Its icon and accessible label describe the destination layout. Manual choices override the automatic build decision and persist through reload and discovery. Switching clears year selection and opens the destination with its usual framing; figures, filters, and Follow a Figure action policies are preserved. The switch is disabled during AI operations and is inert behind dialogs.

The layout switch sits 44 pixels above the bottom edge. A matching zoom-reset button appears beside it whenever either canvas is away from 100% scale. Reset restores 100% and centers the full layout in the canvas area available beside the sidebar and below the header. Gallery uses board bounds; timeline uses the rendered figure and floating-label bounds, excluding empty year-range space. Pointer clicks do not leave a focus ring; keyboard navigation retains its indicator.

Gallery cards share `FigureCard` with the relationship mapper. Cards have fixed 320 × 116 pixel bounds, zero vertical padding, neutral surfaces, and optional 64-pixel portraits. Names and occupations wrap to at most two lines. Discipline uses the legend's category color in a filled 20-pixel triangle at the top right, clipped by the rounded card bounds. Category strips, badges, and hover tooltips are omitted. The focus figure retains its blue border.

Both canvas renderers open an action menu on figure activation. For Follow a Figure builds, the focus figure offers biography and Expand Timeline; other figures offer View Relationship to the focus, biography, and Map Relationships. The same policy applies to sidebar actions. Only the focus may expand the canvas; the action handler also enforces this restriction. Other launcher modes retain their normal available actions. Relationship Mapper's separate source/related-card clicks still open biography/relationship details directly.

Focus resolution prefers an exact normalized name, then a unique whole-word name match (for example, Einstein → Albert Einstein). A saved valid focus ID remains authoritative as discovery adds figures. Legacy Follow a Figure caches with an absent or ambiguous focus retain biography/mapping actions, but disable expansion and relationship-to-focus actions until the focus can be identified. New builds require an unambiguous focus. Opening a relationship still uses the existing evidence assessment and cache; sharing a canvas does not establish a verified connection.

New Follow a Figure builds use the same `assessRelationships` gate as Map Relationships before choosing the layout or committing figures. The focus is required; every other person/event needs a positive verdict with concrete evidence. Explicit rejections and unusable assessments cannot enter the canvas. A failed or cancelled verification preserves the previous canvas. Accepted assessments and relationship IDs are cached for opening explanations and expansion. The launcher shows a connection-checking phase while this runs.

Legacy Follow a Figure caches discard explicit cached rejections on restore without making new AI calls. Previously unassessed candidates are checked when their relationship is opened; a negative verdict removes that candidate from the focus canvas and shows an informational message instead of a relationship dialog. Rebuilding verifies the entire old dataset's replacement before display. Other build modes retain their existing generation behavior.

`calculateGalleryLayout` orders figures into category zones and packs alternating long/short tiers into a roughly landscape-shaped board. Short tiers are centered; equal-length adjacent tiers still stagger by half a card pitch. The viewport does not restrict board bounds. Gallery mouse-wheel impulses and Ctrl/Cmd-wheel pinch zoom around the cursor; fine trackpad scrolling, Shift-wheel, and dragging pan. Two-finger touch gestures both zoom and pan around their centroid. Scale is bounded to 10–500%. Search and keyboard focus reveal cards by panning at the current scale. Discovery repacks the board while preserving the screen position of a nearby existing card at the current zoom. Portrait visibility calculations also account for scale.

Gallery coordinates never replace saved timeline cluster placements. Portraits load for visible cards and nearby cards using the existing Wikipedia cache.
